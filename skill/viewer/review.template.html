How to read this map

Every residential street piece (up to 60 m) is scored 0–100 on each metric. Metrics with a published standard use it (EN 17037 view distance, the BRE 25° daylight rule, WHO noise limits, the 3-30-300 canopy target, Natural England's greenspace ladder, TfL's PTAL bands). The others are ranked within the city and are not comparable between cities.

Everyday access asks whether you can live here without a car. Public transport uses a simplified PTAL: walking time to every stop in reach, plus an assumed waiting time per line, since OpenStreetMap has stops and lines but no timetables. Daily needs is the walking distance to the nearest grocery, café or bar, pharmacy, doctor, school or kindergarten, and library, cinema or gym. The walking distances are design targets, not a standard.

Explore layers shows the evidence behind the scores. Greenery is summer greenness from Sentinel-2. Noise glows along roads in proportion to the estimated level. Sky blocked thickens streets where the opposite buildings take more sky from a first-floor window. Crowds draws people where tourist places cluster.

Noise is a road-class estimate, not a measured map, and it is uncalibrated. Only street-facing views are measured. Courtyard windows, upper floors and single street trees narrower than 10 m are not seen yet.

Tiers: A authoritative local data, B regional or global data at adequate resolution, C a proxy or coarse source.
